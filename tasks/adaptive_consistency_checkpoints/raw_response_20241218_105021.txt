1. **Consequence Consistency**:

2. **Logical Negation (NOT) Consistency**:
   - **Challenging Aspects**: Challenges arise when the negation introduces ambiguities or when the original question has unclear resolution criteria.
   - **Performance Patterns**: Good performance occurs when the negation is a simple, direct opposite. Poor performance stems from overly complex or indirect negations that require nuanced interpretation.
   - **Factors Contributing to Challenges**: Topics that are inherently uncertain or speculative, such as speculative technologies or market predictions, can complicate negations.

   - **Challenging Aspects**: This type is challenging when the paraphrase introduces subtle differences in meaning or when the context of the original question is not fully preserved.
   - **Performance Patterns**: Good performance is noted when the paraphrase precisely mirrors the original question’s intent. Poor performance arises when slight wording changes lead to different interpretations.
   - **Factors Contributing to Challenges**: Highly technical or nuanced topics, where wording precision is crucial, often result in paraphrase difficulties.

4. **Worst-Performing Questions**:
   - Common factors include overly complex dependencies, ambiguous criteria, and speculative topics. Poorly defined resolution criteria and lack of precise language also contribute to low performance across all types.

5. **Common Factors Across Types**:
   - Ambiguity in resolution criteria, complex logical relationships, and speculative or highly variable topics tend to make questions difficult across all consistency types.
